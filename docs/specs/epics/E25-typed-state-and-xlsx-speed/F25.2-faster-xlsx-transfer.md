`rn-forge-data` writes XLSX faster for large tables, and the files it writes
load faster, with no change to what a caller gets back.

## Context

S22.10.3 measured FastAPI XLSX export at about 31 s and import at about 19 s for
100,000 rows × 10 columns, nearly all of it in `rn_forge.data.write_xlsx` and
tablib's XLSX load. S25.2.1 found that the harness's `tracemalloc` inflates
these about 5× and 2.5×: untraced, they are about 6.5 s and 8 s. S22.10.3's
figures are in the
[S22.10.3 baseline](../../../guides/performance-baselines.md#9bcf9b0-s22103-fastapi-transfer-baseline).
The cost sits in the data package, outside route assembly, so
[F22.10](../E22-pykit-simplification/F22.10-fastapi.md) left it out of scope.

## S25.2.1 — Profile XLSX transfer and choose an approach

**State:** Closed (2026-11-18)

**Acceptance:**

- The story records where the time goes for `write_xlsx` and for the XLSX load,
  from a profile at 100,000 × 10, with the profile's commit.
- It compares the candidate approaches, including a write-only or streaming
  workbook and a different reader or writer, by measured time at 100,000 × 10
  and by memory.
- It names the chosen approach and a target time at 100,000 × 10 for export and
  for import, taken from those measurements.
- It states whether any public API changes; when one does, it names the symbols.

Settled contract (owner, 2026-11-18):

- **Where the time goes** (profiled at `5e144716`): export creates a
  `WriteOnlyCell` for every value, and `et_xmlfile` serialises each one. A
  write-only sheet carries no `<dimension>`, so openpyxl's read-only loader
  parses the whole sheet twice on import; the first pass is about a third of
  the import.
- **Approach:** in `write_xlsx` only. Unformatted values are appended raw, and
  `WriteOnlyCell` is created only for columns named in `column_formats`. The
  sheet declares its dimension (`A1:<last column><rows + header>`) before rows
  are appended, through the write-only sheet's `calculate_dimension` hook,
  which openpyxl 3.1.5 reads but does not document; a test guards it.
- **Unchanged:** `write_xlsx`'s signature, header handling, `KeyError` for
  unknown `column_formats` names, and the values read back. tablib still loads
  imports; `rn-forge-fastapi` does not change. No public API changes and no
  new dependency.
- **Target** at 100,000 × 10, untraced, on the S22.10.3 machine, compared
  back-to-back with the unchanged code in one session: export at most 4.8 s,
  import at most 5.5 s.
- **Rejected:** reading without tablib (about 4% faster, and short rows read as
  `None` instead of `''`); lower zip compression (0.1–0.2 s for a 27% larger
  file, through openpyxl internals); `lxml`, `xlsxwriter` and
  `python-calamine` (new dependencies, not measured).

**Outcome:** untraced at `5e144716`, export took 6.1–6.7 s and import 7.9 s; the
prototype took 4.6 s and 5.3 s. Memory was not compared per option; S25.2.2's
before and after harness runs record it.

## S25.2.2 — Implement the faster XLSX path

**State:** Closed (2026-11-18) · **Depends on:** S25.2.1

**Acceptance:**

- The existing S22.10.3 XLSX export and import workloads are re-measured before
  the change and after it, on the same machine and by the same procedure; both
  sets of figures are recorded in the
  [performance baselines](../../../guides/performance-baselines.md).
- The after figures meet the target S25.2.1 set, untraced and compared
  back-to-back with the unchanged code; the harness's peak allocation for each
  case rises by no more than 1%.
- A test asserts that the written sheet declares its `<dimension>`, so an
  openpyxl upgrade that drops the hook fails the gate. The gate has no timing
  assertion.
- Behaviour is preserved: the existing tests pass unchanged, and a test pins
  that a round trip returns the same values and number formats, with and
  without `column_formats`, and for an empty dataset and one without headers.
- The package's `CHANGELOG.md` has an entry under `[Unreleased]`.
- `task validate` passes.

**Outcome:** untraced at 100,000 × 10, `write_xlsx` took 4.40 s (from 6.02 s)
and tablib's load of its output 4.75 s (from 7.06 s); the largest peak
allocation rise was 0.5%, on the 10,000-row FastAPI export.
