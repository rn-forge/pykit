# S22.8.1 — Bound authorization-context retention

Brief for
[S22.8.1](../F22.8-auth.md#s2281-bound-authorization-context-retention), at
`bca98e9`.

## Goal

`AuthorizationContextCache` stops keeping entries after their TTL has passed:
each insert drops the expired entries at the front of an insertion-ordered dict,
so retention is bounded by the accounts resolved within one TTL. Version
invalidation is untouched. The S22.8.4 measurement is re-run and the before and
after recorded.

## Settled

- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: the mechanism is the owner's option B
  from S22.8.4 ([F22.8](../F22.8-auth.md), S22.8.1 acceptance, first bullet,
  and S22.8.4 `Decision`): entries stay in `self._entries`
  (`context_cache.py:128`, a plain `dict`, insertion-ordered); an insert pops
  its own key first, then expires from the front, then inserts. No hard cap,
  no new public parameter, no new class, no second ordering structure
  (rejected as A, C, D in S22.8.4). The public signature, `__all__`, `_Entry`
  and `AuthorizationVersions` are unchanged.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: why pop first. A dict assignment to an
  existing key keeps the old position, so today's
  `self._entries[account_id] = ...` (`context_cache.py:146`) would leave a
  refreshed entry at its old, earlier place and the front sweep would then
  stop at it or expire it by the wrong age.
  `self._entries.pop(account_id, None)` before the sweep makes the new entry
  last. Verified with a draft: the invalidate-then-resolve order becomes
  `["b", "a"]`; today it stays `["a", "b"]`.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: the expiry comparison. `resolve` serves
  an entry only while `now - entry.stored_at < self._ttl`
  (`context_cache.py:139`), so an entry is expired when
  `now - entry.stored_at >= self._ttl`: at exactly the TTL it is expired, as
  `test_change_without_the_api_applies_after_ttl`
  (`tests/test_context_cache.py:83-90`, `clock.now = 10.0`, TTL 10) pins. The
  sweep uses that same `>=`, with the same `now` that `resolve` read at
  `context_cache.py:133` and stores as `stored_at` (`context_cache.py:146`);
  it does not call the clock again.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: where the sweep runs. Only in the miss
  path, inside the existing `with self._lock:` block that stores the entry
  (`context_cache.py:145-146`), in this order: pop the key, expire from the
  front, insert. It does not run on a hit (the hit path takes the lock only to
  read, `context_cache.py:134-135`, and must not gain a write), nor in
  `invalidate`, `invalidate_all`, `set_active` or `bump_session_version` (they
  only bump versions, `context_cache.py:149-177`), and no second lock
  acquisition is added. The sweep stops at the first entry that has not
  expired. Lock acquisitions per call stay as measured (2 on a miss, 1 on a
  hit); the baseline records 32,000 for 16,000 plain accounts.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: the expiry loop deletes while looking at
  the front, so it must not iterate the dict while deleting (that raises
  `RuntimeError`). Use a `while self._entries:` loop that takes
  `oldest = next(iter(self._entries))`, breaks when
  `now - self._entries[oldest].stored_at < self._ttl`, else
  `del self._entries[oldest]`. (This shape was tried in a draft and passes.)
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: a hit does not refresh the TTL, today and
  after (`stored_at` is set only at `context_cache.py:146`; the baseline
  states "A hit does not refresh `stored_at`"). So a hot account is a miss
  every TTL and is then popped and re-inserted at the back; entries stay
  ordered by `stored_at`.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: ordering is by insertion, which equals
  `stored_at` order except between racing threads: `now` is read before the
  provider call and before the lock (`context_cache.py:133`, `144-146`), so
  two threads can insert in the opposite order to their `stored_at`. The sweep
  then stops at an unexpired entry that sits in front of an expired one, which
  stays until it reaches the front. That is accepted: the extra retention is
  bounded by the accounts resolved concurrently within one TTL, so the bound
  below still holds. Do not sort, scan past the first live entry, or take the
  maximum of two clock reads to avoid it.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: the bound, as written in the class
  docstring and the baseline: after any miss completes, the cache holds only
  entries whose accounts were resolved (a miss) within the last TTL and not
  yet swept, i.e. at most the accounts that missed within one TTL (with the
  clock advancing one second per account and a 60 s TTL: 60). It is checked at
  a miss: an idle cache keeps its last entries (expired ones are never served,
  only not yet freed) until the next miss. No hard cap: a burst of N distinct
  accounts inside one TTL retains N.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: version counters are out of scope and
  never pruned or reset. `InMemoryAuthorizationVersions._accounts`
  (`context_cache.py:48`) keeps one counter per account ever bumped; deleting
  one could make an old context current, and the owner parked this as the
  `auth-version-counter-retention` idea
  ([ideas list](../../../ideas/index.md)). Entry removal never touches
  `self._versions`. The invalidated churn case will therefore still retain
  16,000 counters (about 1.18 MB traced in a draft), and the record says so.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: security behaviour is unchanged by
  construction. The versions are read before the provider call
  (`context_cache.py:132`, comment at `142-143`) and stored with the entry, so
  a bump during provider resolution leaves the entry stale; the sweep changes
  neither. Removing an expired entry can only turn a would-be miss into a
  miss: an expired entry is never served (`context_cache.py:139`).
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: single-flight (one provider call for
  concurrent misses of one account) is not added. The measurement shows no
  case for it: the sweep adds no lock acquisition and no provider call (the
  hot case keeps its 21,340 provider calls in the draft run). Concurrent
  misses of one account still each call the provider, as today.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: the documented bound lives in the
  `AuthorizationContextCache` class docstring (contract only: entries are
  served for at most *ttl* and kept no longer than the next miss after that;
  the number held is bounded by the accounts that missed within one *ttl*;
  version counters are not pruned) and, with the measured figures, in
  `docs/guides/performance-baselines.md`. No ADR and no architecture page.
- Settled by the reviewer, 2026-11-16: the baseline harness is
  `packages/rn-forge-auth/tests/measure/test_context_churn.py` (5 cases: plain
  1,000, 4,000, 16,000; invalidated 16,000; hot 16,000 steps over 20
  accounts). It reads `cache._lock`, `cache._entries` and
  `cache._versions._accounts` (lines 91, 99, 123); the change keeps those
  names and types, so the harness is not edited. Keep `self._lock` a
  `threading.Lock` that is replaced as a whole (the harness swaps it).

## Current behaviour

At `bca98e9`:

- `resolve` (`context_cache.py:130-147`) reads the versions, then `now`, then
  looks the entry up under `self._lock`; an entry is a hit when its versions
  equal the current ones and `now - stored_at < ttl`. On a miss it calls the
  provider outside the lock, then stores `_Entry(context, versions, now)`
  under the lock. Nothing ever removes an entry: a second miss of the same
  account overwrites in place; an account never requested again stays forever.
- Measured in S22.8.4 (`performance-baselines.md:345-399`, five runs,
  2026-11-16, 60 s TTL, clock +1 s per account): 16,000 distinct accounts
  leave 16,000 entries (60 live), 6,787,846 B retained, peak 6,846,758 B, lock
  hold total 0.021412113 s and max 0.000050791 s (medians). Hot: 16,020
  entries, 21,340 provider calls. Invalidated: 16,000 entries and 16,000
  version counters, 7,202,870 B retained.
- The characterization test is
  `test_entries_of_accounts_no_longer_requested_are_retained_today`
  (`tests/test_context_cache.py:138-152`): 2,000 distinct accounts at one
  second apart leave `len(cache._entries) == 2000`, 60 of them younger than 60
  s, and `resolve("a0")` leaves 2,000. It says "S22.8.1 replaces this".
- Guards that exist and pass today, all in `tests/test_context_cache.py`:
  `test_invalidate_misses_only_that_account` (63),
  `test_invalidate_all_misses_every_account` (73),
  `test_change_without_the_api_applies_after_ttl` (83),
  `test_set_active_is_seen_by_next_resolve` (93: the account becomes
  `active is False`, which is the cache-level inactive-account rejection),
  `test_bump_session_version_is_seen_by_next_resolve` (100),
  `test_shared_versions_invalidate_across_caches` (126). The Django adapter's
  cache use is covered by
  `packages/rn-forge-django/tests/auth/test_authorization.py`
  (`test_signals_bump_affected_versions`,
  `test_viewset_changes_bump_authorization`) and need not run for this change.
- No existing test covers a version bump during provider resolution. That test
  is missing and is added below.

## Change

- **Change:** in `packages/rn-forge-auth/src/rn_forge/auth/context_cache.py`,
  replace the store at `context_cache.py:145-146` with, under the same lock:
  `self._entries.pop(account_id, None)`; the front-expiry loop settled above
  (using `now` and `self._ttl`, `>=` is expired); then
  `self._entries[account_id] = _Entry(context, versions, now)`. Add a short
  comment saying why the pop precedes the sweep and the insert (a dict
  overwrite keeps its old position). Extend the class docstring with the bound
  (contract only, no history). `AuthorizationContextCache.__init__`,
  `invalidate`, `invalidate_all`, `set_active`, `bump_session_version` and
